TRADITIONAL MISO-GLAZED COD
(GINDARA SAIKYO-YAKI)

Silky black cod fillets marinated in sweet Saikyo miso paste, then broiled until deeply caramelized. A classic Japanese miso cod recipe that brings restaurant-quality flavor to your home kitchen.

⏱ PREP: 10 minutes   MARINATE: 24–72 hours
COOK: 12 minutes

INSTRUCTIONS

  1. Pat the cod fillets completely dry with paper towels. Remove any pin bones with tweezers. Thorough drying helps the marinade adhere properly.
  2. In a small bowl, whisk together the Saikyo miso paste, mirin, sake, and sugar until smooth and fully combined into a uniform glaze.
  3. Coat each fillet thoroughly on all sides with the miso marinade. Place in a zip-lock bag or airtight container, skin-side down, and refrigerate for at least 24 hours—48 to 72 hours is ideal for the deepest flavor penetration.
  4. When ready to cook, set your oven to broil on high, or preheat to 400°F (200°C) with the broil element on. Lightly oil a foil-lined baking sheet or oven-safe skillet.
  5. Remove the fillets from the marinade and use your fingers or a spoon to gently scrape off any thick excess miso—leaving too much will cause burning, not caramelizing.
  6. Place fillets skin-side down on the prepared pan. Broil 6 inches from the heat source for 10–12 minutes, until the surface is deeply golden and caramelized and the fish flakes easily. Watch closely after the 8-minute mark.
  7. Rest the fillets for 2 minutes. Serve over steamed rice, garnished with sliced scallions and pickled ginger.

CHEF'S TIPS

  • Saikyo miso is the key: Kyoto-style Saikyo miso is sweeter and milder than regular white miso. It's worth sourcing from a Japanese grocery store—the caramelization is noticeably better.
  • Don't rush the marinate: The traditional technique requires patience. A full 48–72 hours allows the miso's enzymes to tenderize the fish and infuse deep umami flavor.
  • Scrape off the excess: This is the most common mistake. Any thick miso left on the surface will burn black under the broiler before the fish is cooked through.
  • Watch the broiler closely: The sugar in the marinade caramelizes quickly. Stay near the oven after the 8-minute mark—30 seconds can be the difference between perfectly glazed and burnt.
  • Room temperature before cooking: Take the fillets out of the fridge 15 minutes before broiling for more even cooking.
